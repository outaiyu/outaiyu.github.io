📜 Paper Making

From Ancient China to Modern Day

Our Daily Paper

We use many kinds of paper in our daily lives, such as notebook paper, tissues, cardboard boxes, and paper bags. Most of them are made from trees.

🏛️ History Timeline

105 AD

Paper was invented in China by Cai Lun during the Han Dynasty. He created a method using mulberry bark, hemp, and old rags.

751 AD

The Battle of Talas led to the spread of papermaking to the Islamic world, where it flourished and improved.

12th Century

Papermaking reached Europe through Spain and Italy, revolutionizing communication and record-keeping.

1440 AD

Johannes Gutenberg's printing press dramatically increased paper demand, leading to mass production techniques.

19th Century

Wood pulp paper was developed, making paper cheaper and more accessible to everyone.

Today

Modern paper production uses sustainable practices and recycling to protect our forests.

🌳 What's Inside Trees?

Inside trees, there are long, thin plant fibers. These fibers are made mostly of a natural material called cellulose. Cellulose makes the fibers strong and flexible—like tiny threads that can intertwine with one another.

💡 The Paper Making Process

When people make paper, they break the fibers apart, mix them with water to make pulp, then spread the pulp flat and press it dry. The way the fibers are arranged affects how strong or soft the paper turns out.

📋 Different Types of Paper

Different kinds of paper are made from different fibers and in different ways. Hover over each type to learn more!

📦 Cardboard

Thick and tough because its fibers are long and tightly bonded. Perfect for packaging and protection.

📓 Notebook Paper

Medium-weight paper designed for writing, with smooth texture and good ink absorption.

🧻 Tissue Paper

Soft and delicate because its fibers are more loosely arranged and often shorter. Gentle and absorbent.

🛍️ Paper Bags

Durable and foldable, made with medium-length fibers for strength and flexibility.
